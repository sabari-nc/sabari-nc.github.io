**Engineering geologist specialising in multi-trigger slope failure mechanisms and their impact on mountain infrastructure resilience.**

I recently completed my PhD at the [Centre for Disaster Mitigation and Management](https://vit.ac.in/centers/cdmm/), [Vellore Institute of Technology (VIT)](https://vit.ac.in/), under [Dr. G. P. Ganapathy](https://vitdirectory.vit.ac.in/page/faculty/11345). My doctoral research examined the geophysical and geospatial dimensions of landslide dynamics — from natural causative factors to vehicle-induced ground vibrations — along landslide–transportation corridors in the Nilgiris, Southern India.

I hold an M.Sc. in Applied Geology from the University of Madras and a B.Sc. in Geology from Thiruvalluvar University. Along the way, I've worked as a Teaching cum Research Assistant at VIT, and interned in earthquake geophysics at the Wadia Institute of Himalayan Geology and in hydrogeology at the Central Ground Water Board.

**What drives my research:**

Mountain roads and railways don't just cross landslide terrain — they interact with it. My work sits at that intersection, asking questions that matter for the millions who depend on these corridors:

- **Beyond rainfall:** Landslides in the Western Ghats are conventionally read as rainfall events. I combine geophysics, field instrumentation, and spatial analysis to uncover the less visible triggers — subsurface conditions, anthropogenic loading, and traffic-induced vibrations — that precondition slopes to fail.
- **Susceptibility maps people can act on:** A map is only useful if it informs a decision. I develop corridor-scale, machine learning–driven susceptibility models built on long-term maintenance inventories, designed for the engineers and agencies who maintain these routes.
- **Hazards to infrastructure, not just terrain:** Quantifying what a landslide costs a road, a railway, or a lifeline structure — so mitigation investment goes where it prevents the most disruption.
- **From one corridor to many:** Extending these methods across geological hazards and geographies, keeping the research firmly application-oriented.
